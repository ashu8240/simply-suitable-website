How to read a mutual fund factsheet, with a made-up example

12 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

A factsheet is a short monthly summary of a mutual fund scheme, published by the fund house. It is a good starting point for understanding a scheme, as long as you know what each part is telling you.

The short version

  • A factsheet is a summary. The scheme documents carry the full details, and you should read them before investing.
  • Check the objective, the risk level, the costs and the portfolio against your own goal and comfort.
  • Read the performance table with care. It describes the past and does not predict the future.

Reading the performance table

Factsheets show how a scheme did over past periods, usually next to its benchmark. A few points help you read it fairly:

  • Look at the period. A strong one-year figure may follow a weak stretch, or the reverse.
  • Compare the scheme with its own benchmark and with schemes of the same category, not with a different kind of scheme.
  • Remember that the table describes the past. It says nothing certain about what comes next.

Past performance may or may not be sustained in future and is not a guarantee of any future returns. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully.

Questions to ask while reading

  • Does the objective match my goal and my time horizon?
  • Does the riskometer match my risk profile? You can find your risk profile to help with this.
  • What are the costs, and is there an exit load?
  • What does the portfolio hold, and am I comfortable with it?

Where to find it

Each fund house publishes factsheets every month on its website. The portfolio shown is a snapshot at the end of the month, so it changes from month to month as the fund manager buys and sells.

Words to know

AUM
Assets under management: the total money a scheme manages.
Benchmark
An index used as a yardstick for judging a scheme.
Exit load
A charge some schemes levy if you sell within a set period.
Riskometer
The scale showing a scheme’s risk level, from Low to Very High.
Asset allocation
How a scheme splits its money between shares, bonds, cash and other assets.

Common questions

Is a factsheet the same as the scheme documents?

No. The factsheet is a monthly summary. The scheme information document and the key information memorandum carry the full details, and you should read them before investing.

Does a factsheet tell me whether to invest?

No. It gives you facts about the scheme. Whether it suits you depends on your goal, your time horizon and your comfort with risk.

Why does the portfolio change every month?

The fund manager buys and sells holdings, and the factsheet shows a snapshot at month-end.
