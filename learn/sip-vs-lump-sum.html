SIP or lump sum? How to think about the choice

11 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

A lump sum means investing a whole amount at once. A SIP means investing the same total in regular instalments. Both buy units of the same kinds of schemes. The difference is when your money goes in.

The short version

  • Neither method is always better. Which one comes out ahead depends on how prices move, and nobody can know that in advance.
  • A SIP fits income that arrives monthly and spreads your purchase dates. A lump sum fits money you already have, but puts all of it in at one price.
  • The more important questions are about your goal, your time horizon and how you feel about ups and downs.

How they differ

Lump sumSIP
How money goes inAll at onceIn regular instalments
Where the money usually comes fromMoney you already have, such as savings or a bonusMonthly income
Purchase priceOne NAVSeveral NAVs

A hypothetical example

Suppose you have ₹30,000 to invest. In one case you invest all of it on the first date. In the other you invest ₹10,000 on each of three dates. Here are two made-up price paths.

Price pathNAV on the three datesNAV at the endLump sum is worthSIP is worth
A: rises steadily₹10, ₹11, ₹12₹12₹36,000₹32,909
B: dips, then recovers₹10, ₹8, ₹9₹11₹33,000₹36,972

In path A, putting everything in on day one came out ahead, because all of the money benefited from the early low price. In path B, the SIP came out ahead, because the dip let it buy more units. Nobody knows in advance which path will happen.

These NAVs are made up to show the arithmetic. They do not come from any scheme, and they say nothing about future returns. Past performance may or may not be sustained in future and is not a guarantee of any future returns.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Do I have a lump sum now, or does my money arrive month by month?
  • When will I need this money?
  • How would I feel if the value fell soon after I invested all of it?
  • Do I want a regular habit that runs by itself?
  • Could I keep a SIP going through a month when money is tight?

Other approaches

Some people invest a lump sum in stages over several months. The same logic applies: it spreads out the purchase dates, but it does not remove risk. And trying to pick the best moment to invest in advance is something no one can promise to do.

You can also combine methods, for example a regular SIP from your salary and a one-time investment when you receive a bonus.

Common questions

Is a SIP safer than a lump sum?

A SIP spreads when you buy, which many people find more comfortable. It does not make the scheme itself less risky. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks.

Can I do both?

Yes. Whether that suits you depends on your goals and your cash flow.

Which one is right for me?

It depends on your goal, your time horizon, your cash flow and your comfort with ups and downs. A short conversation can help you think it through.
