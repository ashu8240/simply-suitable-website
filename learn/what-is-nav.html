What NAV means, and what it doesn’t

11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

NAV stands for Net Asset Value. It is the price of one unit of a mutual fund scheme on a given day.

The short version

  • NAV is the value of everything a scheme holds, minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units.
  • A lower NAV does not make a scheme cheaper, and a higher NAV does not make it expensive. What matters is how the NAV changes, not where it starts.
  • You buy and sell units at the NAV that applies to your transaction.

How NAV is worked out

The scheme adds up the market value of everything it holds, including cash. It then subtracts its liabilities and expenses, and divides what is left by the total number of units held by investors. For most schemes this is done on every business day.

Here is a made-up example.

Market value of holdings and cash₹50,05,000
Less: liabilities and expenses₹5,000
Net assets₹50,00,000
Units held by all investors5,00,000
NAV per unit₹10.00

Why NAV matters to you

  • When you invest, the amount you put in is divided by the applicable NAV to give you units. ₹10,000 at a NAV of ₹10 buys 1,000 units.
  • The value of your holding on any day is your units multiplied by that day’s NAV.
  • The NAV that applies to your transaction depends on when your request and money are received and on the scheme’s rules, so check the scheme documents.

A low NAV is not a bargain

It is tempting to think a scheme with a NAV of ₹10 is cheaper than one with a NAV of ₹100. It isn’t. Suppose you put ₹10,000 into each, and each NAV then rises by 10%.

Scheme AScheme B
NAV when you invest₹10₹100
Units bought with ₹10,0001,000100
NAV after a 10% rise₹11₹110
Value of your holding₹11,000₹11,000

The number of units differs, but the result is the same. What counts is the percentage change in the NAV over the time you hold, not the level it started at.

These schemes and numbers are made up to show the arithmetic. They say nothing about any real scheme or about future returns. Past performance may or may not be sustained in future and is not a guarantee of any future returns.

Where to find NAV

NAVs are published each business day on the AMFI website and on the website of each fund house. Your account statement also shows the units you hold and the NAV used for each transaction.

What NAV does not tell you

  • It does not show how risky a scheme is.
  • It does not show how a scheme compares with another one that has a different NAV.
  • On its own, it says nothing about what the scheme will do next.

Words to know

Unit
The share of a scheme you own. You get units when you invest.
Expense ratio
The yearly cost of running a scheme, shown as a percentage of its assets. It is deducted from the scheme’s assets, so the NAV you see is already after expenses.
Plan and option
A scheme can have several plans (such as regular and direct) and options (such as growth and IDCW). Each has its own NAV.

Common questions

Does NAV change every day?

For most open-ended schemes, yes. It follows the value of the holdings, which moves with the markets.

Is a higher NAV better?

No. The level of a NAV does not show quality or value. Judge a scheme on its objective, its risk level and how it fits your goal, not on whether its NAV looks high or low.

Why do I see more than one NAV for the same scheme?

Because each plan and option, such as regular or direct, growth or IDCW, has its own NAV.
