Why an emergency fund comes before investing

11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

An emergency fund is money set aside only for surprises: a medical bill, a sudden repair, a gap in income. It sits apart from your investments, so a bad month doesn’t force you to sell them.

The short version

  • An emergency fund protects your investments, because you don’t have to sell them at a bad time to pay for a surprise.
  • A common rule of thumb is to keep a few months of essential expenses. The right number depends on your situation.
  • Keep it somewhere you can reach quickly, and where its value is not exposed to market ups and downs.

Why it comes first

Investments that carry market risk can be down at the very moment a surprise arrives. If you have no cushion, you may have to sell when the value is low, which turns a temporary dip into a real loss. A cushion lets your investments stay invested for the goal they were meant for.

How much do you need?

  1. Add up your essential monthly expenses. Rent or EMI, groceries, utilities, school fees, insurance premiums and minimum loan payments.
  2. Choose a number of months. A common rule of thumb is three to six months. People with irregular income, such as business owners or commission earners, or people with dependents, often choose more.
  3. Multiply. For example, essential expenses of ₹40,000 a month and a target of six months gives ₹2,40,000.

That figure is only an example to show the arithmetic. It is not a target for you.

Where to keep it

What matters is that you can reach the money quickly and that its value won’t fall when markets do. The aim is availability, not growth. Many people keep it in a savings account, separate from the account they spend from.

How to build one

  1. Work out your number using the steps above.
  2. Keep the money in a separate account, or label it clearly, so it isn’t spent by accident.
  3. Start with a small first milestone, such as one month of essential expenses.
  4. Set up an automatic transfer on the day your income arrives.
  5. Raise the target step by step until you reach your number.

Some people build the cushion first. Others build it alongside a small SIP. Either way, avoid investing money you may need in the near term.

When to use it, and what to do afterwards

Use it for genuine, unplanned essentials. A sale, a holiday or a planned purchase is not an emergency. After you use it, top it up again before you increase other spending or investing.

Words to know

Emergency fund
Money kept aside only for unplanned, essential expenses.
Liquidity
How quickly you can turn money into cash without losing value.
Essential expenses
The costs you must meet every month, such as housing, food, utilities, school fees and loan payments.

Common questions

Should my emergency fund be invested in mutual funds?

Many people keep it separate from market-linked investments, because those can be down when you need the money. If you want to talk about what suits your situation, get in touch.

What if I have loans?

Loan payments count as essential expenses, so they are part of the number you work out.

I already invest. Is it too late to build one?

No. You can build it alongside your investments, starting with a small first target.
