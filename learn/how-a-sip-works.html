How a SIP works, in plain language

10 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way to invest in a mutual fund scheme in regular instalments instead of one large amount. You choose an amount and a date, and that amount is invested automatically at the interval you set.

The short version

  • A SIP is a method of investing, not a product. The risk comes from the scheme you invest in.
  • Each instalment buys units at the NAV on that day: more units when the NAV is lower, fewer when it is higher.
  • A SIP builds a habit and spreads your purchase dates. It does not guarantee a profit or protect you from a loss.

What happens when you start a SIP

  1. You choose the details. A scheme, an amount, how often (monthly is the most common) and a date.
  2. You set up a standing instruction. Your bank is told it may debit that amount on the chosen dates.
  3. Each instalment is invested at that day’s NAV. NAV is the price of one unit of the scheme. You are allotted units for the amount invested.
  4. Your units build up over time. The value of what you hold moves up and down with the NAV.

Your KYC must be complete before you invest. Minimum amounts, dates and timings differ by scheme and platform, so check the scheme documents for the details that apply to you.

A simple example

Suppose you invest ₹5,000 a month for three months, and the NAV on the three dates is ₹10, ₹8 and ₹12.50.

MonthAmountNAVUnits bought
1₹5,000₹10.00500.00
2₹5,000₹8.00625.00
3₹5,000₹12.50400.00
Total₹15,0001,525.00

Because the NAV fell in month 2, the same ₹5,000 bought more units. Your average cost per unit (₹15,000 ÷ 1,525 units) is about ₹9.84, a little lower than the plain average of the three NAVs, which is about ₹10.17. This effect is often called rupee cost averaging. It describes how your purchase cost averages out. It does not mean you will make a profit.

These NAVs are made up to show the arithmetic. They do not come from any scheme, and they say nothing about future returns. Past performance may or may not be sustained in future and is not a guarantee of any future returns.

What a SIP can do

  • Turn investing into a regular habit, tied to your income cycle.
  • Let you start with a smaller amount than a single large investment would need.
  • Spread your purchases over different dates, so you are not relying on one day’s price.

What a SIP cannot do

  • Guarantee returns, or remove market risk.
  • Make a scheme suitable for you if its risk level doesn’t match your comfort or your time horizon.
  • Replace an emergency cushion. If you have to stop or withdraw at a bad time, the value may be lower than what you put in.
  • Prevent a fall in value. While the market is down, your units can show a loss on paper.

Questions to ask before you start

  • What is this money for, and when will I need it?
  • Does the risk level of the scheme suit me? You can find your risk profile to help you think about this.
  • Could I keep the SIP going if my income dropped for a few months?
  • Do I have money set aside for emergencies first?
  • Do I know how to change or stop the SIP if I need to?

Words to know

SIP
Systematic Investment Plan: a standing instruction to invest a fixed amount at regular intervals.
NAV
Net Asset Value: the price of one unit of a scheme on a given day.
Unit
The share of a scheme you own. You buy units when you invest, and your holding is units multiplied by the NAV.
Scheme
A specific mutual fund offering, with its own objective, risk level and documents.

Common questions

Can I stop or pause a SIP?

In general, yes. The process and any notice period differ between fund houses and platforms, so check the scheme documents and your platform’s rules.

What if an instalment fails?

Rules vary. The instalment may be skipped, and repeated failures may lead to the SIP being cancelled. Read the terms that apply to you.

Is a SIP safe?

A SIP is a way of investing. How safe your money is depends on the scheme you choose, and mutual fund investments are subject to market risks.

How much should I invest each month?

There is no single right amount. It depends on your goals, your time horizon and your monthly cash flow. A short conversation can help you think it through.
