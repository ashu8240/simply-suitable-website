What a risk profile is, and why it matters

11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

A risk profile is a picture of how much investment risk you can afford to take, and how much you are comfortable taking. It helps match what you invest in with your own situation, not with what is popular.

The short version

  • A risk profile has two parts: your ability to take risk and your willingness to take it.
  • Every scheme has its own risk level, shown on a riskometer. Your profile helps you see which levels may suit you.
  • It is a snapshot. It can change when your life changes.

Two sides of risk

Ability is about your circumstances: how stable your income is, how long until you need the money, what loans and responsibilities you carry, and whether you have savings set aside for emergencies.

Willingness is about your feelings: how you would react if the value of an investment fell sharply, and whether you would stay put or sell.

The two do not always match. Someone with a steady income and a long time horizon may still be very uncomfortable with falls. Someone relaxed about ups and downs may have a short time horizon and a large loan. When they differ, it is usually sensible to be guided by the more cautious of the two.

What the riskometer shows

Every mutual fund scheme displays a riskometer showing its risk level. There are six levels:

  1. Low
  2. Low to Moderate
  3. Moderate
  4. Moderately High
  5. High
  6. Very High

The level describes the risk of what the scheme holds, and it is reviewed from time to time, so it can change. A riskometer tells you about the scheme. A risk profile tells you about you. You need both to judge whether a scheme fits.

What goes into a risk profile

A good questionnaire asks about your situation and your reactions, not only whether you think of yourself as cautious or bold. Typical topics include:

  • Your time horizon for each goal.
  • How stable and predictable your income is.
  • Your loans and other commitments.
  • What you already hold.
  • How you reacted to falls in the past.

Why your profile can change

A new job, a marriage, a child, a loan, or getting closer to a goal can all change the answer. That is why a risk profile is worth revisiting, and not filling in once and forgetting.

What a risk profile is not

  • It is not a prediction of returns.
  • It is not a guarantee of how you will feel in a real fall.
  • It is not a recommendation to buy a particular scheme.

If you’d like to see where you stand, you can find your risk profile with the short questionnaire. It is optional and takes about five minutes.

Words to know

Time horizon
How long until you need the money for a goal.
Risk ability
How much risk your circumstances allow you to take.
Risk willingness
How much risk you are comfortable taking.
Riskometer
The scale on a scheme that shows its risk level, from Low to Very High.

Common questions

Is a risk profile a test I can pass or fail?

No. There are no right answers. The aim is an honest picture of you today.

How often should I update it?

When something significant changes in your life, and as you get closer to a goal. A regular review is also sensible.

Can my risk profile differ for different goals?

Yes. A goal that is many years away sits differently from one that is two years away, which is why the time horizon of each goal matters.
